# Visual Atlas {#sec:visual-atlas}

This section is called a visual atlas rather than results because the paper does
not run experiments. The figures are conceptual artifacts generated from the
static source inventory. They visualize the argument developed in [@sec:method]
and are formally indexed in [@sec:formal-supplement].

## Tinbergen Axes

[@fig:tinbergen_axes] shows the central correction. Tinbergen's four questions
form two axes, not a stacked series of causes. The quadrant that creates regress
pressure is ontogeny when it is made to answer more than it can answer.

![Tinbergen's four questions form two crossed axes (proximate/ultimate × static/developmental); mechanism and function close locally, ontogeny defers, phylogeny blocks regress through selection-filtered persistence.](../output/figures/tinbergen_axes.png){#fig:tinbergen_axes}

## Hyperprior Regress And Termination

[@fig:regress_terminations] maps the formal parallel between hierarchical Bayes
and developmental inheritance. A prior on a prior can always be extended upward.
A developmental prior that selects another developmental prior can always be
extended backward. The explanatory task is not to add another level; it is to
name the stopping condition.

![A conceptual flow from prior to prior-of-prior to further hyperprior levels, with the explanatory task located in naming where it stops rather than in adding another level.](../output/figures/regress_terminations.png){#fig:regress_terminations}

## Prior As Settled Equilibrium

[@fig:prior_equilibrium_map] sketches the fixed-point move. In this framing, a
prior is not an object inside the system that needs another prior above it. It
is the settled organization of perception, action, and boundary maintenance that
persists. This is where the argument sits closest to free-energy and
autopoietic readings of cognition [@friston2010free; @maturana1980autopoiesis].

![A circular equilibrium map showing blanket persistence, action-perception closure, environmental viability, and a central settled prior-like form written as the fixed point X* = Phi(X*).](../output/figures/prior_equilibrium_map.png){#fig:prior_equilibrium_map}

## Ontogeny And Hyperprior Shape

[@fig:ontogeny_hyperprior_parallel] makes the structural analogy explicit. In
hierarchical Bayes, a prior can be justified by a hyperprior, which can be
justified by another hyperprior. In an ontogenetic-only biological account, a
current prior can be justified by a developmental selector, which can then be
asked to justify its own source. The point is not that these are the same
substantive theory. The point is that they share a regress shape.

![Two parallel chains compare hierarchical Bayes and ontogeny-only explanation. The upper chain moves from prior to hyperprior to hyper-hyperprior. The lower chain moves from current prior to developmental selector to the source of that selector. A red bracket marks the same regress shape.](../output/figures/ontogeny_hyperprior_parallel.png){#fig:ontogeny_hyperprior_parallel}

## Selection Filter

[@fig:selection_filter] distinguishes selection termination from merely adding
another historical layer. Function and phylogeny do not explain persistence by
placing one more prior behind the prior. They filter the space of candidate
forms: fragile, overfit, or miscalibrated priors do not remain available as the
objects whose origin we ask about.

![Candidate priors pass through a function-plus-phylogeny viability filter. Nonviable priors fade out, while the persisting prior exits as the form available for explanation.](../output/figures/selection_filter.png){#fig:selection_filter}

## Policy Entanglement Bridge

[@fig:policy_entanglement_bridge] sketches why the argument sits beside
policy-entanglement work. If lambda-decomposition extracts policy, state, and
prior-like factors from an already joint structure, then those factors are useful
cuts rather than proof that the factors were ontologically first. This is the
same explanatory posture as fixed-point termination: organization can precede
the decomposition that later makes it legible.

![A joint organization node feeds lambda-decomposition, which then separates policy, prior-like constraint, and state factor. A caption line notes that useful cuts do not prove that factors came first.](../output/figures/policy_entanglement_bridge.png){#fig:policy_entanglement_bridge}

## Niche Co-Construction

[@fig:niche_construction_diagram] shows how the prior-like structure is
co-shaped by organism and environment. The niche-construction reading does not
eliminate selection — viability still filters what can persist — but it shows
that the filter is not purely external. The prior is not fully given by the
environment nor fully endogenous; it is co-shaped by the reciprocal interaction
[@constant2018free].

![A reciprocal diagram showing organism and environment co-shaping a prior through niche construction, written as N(P) <-> P.](../output/figures/niche_construction_diagram.png){#fig:niche_construction_diagram}

## Markov Blanket As Achievement

[@fig:markov_blanket_achievement] shows the Markov blanket as a
self-maintaining model boundary rather than an externally imposed partition.
When the blanket is treated as an achievement of the system, the prior-like
structure becomes part of what the model describes the system as maintaining,
not a parameter received from a higher level [@kirchhoff2018markov;
@friston2013life]. The figure also marks the scope boundary introduced by
recent critiques: the notation is not enough by itself to settle organismal or
cognitive ontology [@bruineberg2022emperor; @raja2021markov].

![The Markov blanket as a model-level boundary achieved by the settled system process, with a warning that organismal ontology is not read directly from notation.](../output/figures/markov_blanket_achievement.png){#fig:markov_blanket_achievement}

## Explanatory Complementarity

[@fig:explanatory_complementarity_matrix] shows the four Tinbergen questions as
complementary perspectives rather than rival hypotheses. The regress appears
when one question is asked to carry the load of all four; the framework itself
does not force it [@bateson2004toward].

![A 4x4 matrix showing that the four Tinbergen questions complement rather than compete with each other; each diagonal cell is one question asked of the same phenomenon.](../output/figures/explanatory_complementarity_matrix.png){#fig:explanatory_complementarity_matrix}

## Active Inference Cycle

[@fig:active_inference_cycle] shows how active inference closes the
action-perception loop. The prior serves a process that minimizes free energy
through both inference and action, not inference alone
[@friston2015active; @da_costa2020active]. This closes the explanatory loop:
the prior is not merely a parameter for belief updating but a constraint on the
coupled perception-action system.

![A circular cycle showing perception, inference, action, and policy selection as four stages of the free-energy-minimizing loop.](../output/figures/active_inference_cycle.png){#fig:active_inference_cycle}

## Variational Free Energy Landscape

[@fig:variational_free_energy_landscape] shows the free-energy landscape as a
surface the system settles into. The prior-like structure is the settling point
of the variational bound, not an external parameter
[@friston2010free; @friston2013life]. The zero-energy limit is the exact
posterior, anchoring the prior to the system's own inference.

![A conceptual free-energy landscape with a basin; the prior is marked as the settling point P at the bottom where F* ~ 0.](../output/figures/variational_free_energy_landscape.png){#fig:variational_free_energy_landscape}

## Scale Invariance

[@fig:scale_invariance_diagram] shows that the regress-termination problem
recurs at multiple scales. The fixed-point move applies at the cellular,
organismic, and social levels without requiring a new hidden prior at each
transition [@friston2013life; @kirchhoff2018markov]. The same category shift
applies recursively: the prior-like settlement at one scale becomes the
substrate for the next.

![Three stacked panels showing the same fixed-point structure at cellular, organismic, and social scales, connected by the scale recursion operator R.](../output/figures/scale_invariance_diagram.png){#fig:scale_invariance_diagram}

## Source-Claim Boundary Map

[@fig:source_claim_boundary_map] makes the scholarship contract visible. The
publication pass does not treat every discovered source as equal. Perplexity
surfaces candidates; primary or scholarly verification decides whether a source
is accepted, rejected, or deferred; and only accepted sources attached to claim
ledger entries reach the manuscript. This is the paper's answer to citation
inflation: sources enter only where they bound or strengthen a live claim.

![A source-to-claim map showing Perplexity-discovered scholarly source families flowing through accepted-source and rejected/deferred layers before reaching the claim ledger, manuscript claims, formal inventory, and visual atlas.](../output/figures/source_claim_boundary_map.png){#fig:source_claim_boundary_map}

## Markov Blanket Scope Split

[@fig:markov_blanket_scope_split] separates three uses of Markov blankets:
Pearl-style conditional-independence structure, Friston-style active-inference
model boundary, and realized organismal or cognitive boundary. The split lets
the argument use Markov blankets as part of fixed-point explanation while
preserving the critical distinction emphasized by the recent debate
[@bruineberg2022emperor; @raja2021markov].

![A three-layer scope diagram separating Pearl blankets as conditional-independence tools, Friston blankets as active-inference model boundaries, and realized biological boundaries that require additional assumptions.](../output/figures/markov_blanket_scope_split.png){#fig:markov_blanket_scope_split}

## Niche-Prior Feedback Field

[@fig:niche_prior_feedback_field] gives the niche-construction claim a richer
shape. Organismic action, developmental resources, and cultural inheritance
operate at different timescales but feed back into the same prior-like field.
The point is not that niche construction is a fourth termination. It is a
constraint on selection termination: what persists is partly produced by the
system-niche loop that selection later filters [@constant2018free;
@stotz2017developmental; @baltieri2022extended].

![A layered feedback field showing organismic action, developmental resources, and cultural inheritance co-shaping a central prior-like field across timescales.](../output/figures/niche_prior_feedback_field.png){#fig:niche_prior_feedback_field}
